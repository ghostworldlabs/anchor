# Stewards that answer while the work runs

John waited 30 to 46 minutes (median) for replies from the Cave of Wonders, the Star Chart and the MBA Teaching cockpit, and wrote "Are you still really working?" while his messages queued behind one turn. The Codex threads showed why: the steward did the code work itself inside the conversation turn, and about nine in ten of its steps were its own ceremony (status-file writes, a plan page, one-off verify and record scripts, a report) for what were small UI changes. Each step took about 50 seconds over a resent context of about 135k tokens, while its reasoning per step was small. The model and effort (gpt-6.1-sol at ultra) were not the cause, and John ruled out lowering them for hard problems. Diagnosis in Ecgberht journals 2069 and 2070; baseline in Ecgberht/planning/steward-responsiveness-2026-10-04/BASELINE-2026-10-04.txt.

What changed in Anchor (809a392, plan v0.2 approved by John), for every steward surface because one engine serves them all. A message sent mid-turn gets a receipt naming what the steward is on and since when, with Take this now, which ends the turn and delivers his message at once (`take_now`, the same interrupt-then-wake the self-started branch already used). The steward's contract now leads with a small-change duty: the edit, one check, a commit, a sentence; the effort rules (slice loop, plans forward, deliverables register, plan drift) ride only in effort mode, which his words set ("make this an effort", "small mode") or a cockpit with a step in progress, read fresh each turn. A small-mode turn past 15 tool calls is ended and handed off. Real work goes to a code job: `tools/steward_code_job.py start` makes a worktree of the owning repository under %LOCALAPPDATA%/anchor-code-jobs, runs a workbench-terminal engine there on the same latest-model, highest-effort policy, checks and commits its work, merges it only when the repository has no uncommitted edits to the same files and no merge in progress, and wakes the steward with the result as a control turn. One writer per repository across every steward (a global busy registry, path-boundary matching, shell writes included); "stop the coder" kills the job and keeps its branch. `tools/turn_profile.py` measures his wait from the conversation log and the steps from the provider threads.

Verified: 21 new hermetic tests (temp git repos, fake coders, the real report path, concurrent starts); the steward-engine suites match master's 52 pre-existing failures exactly; Grok reviewed the code four rounds (7, 3 and 2 findings, then SOUND), including a blocker where a coder's own commit or a git timeout would have deleted its work. Live after an idle-checked restart: the code_job route and the tool answer from the Cave's folder, and Take this now refuses safely when nothing waits. Not yet shown: faster replies on his record; rerun turn_profile after a day of use against the baseline.

Two process notes. The Bash heredoc halved backslashes four times during the build (regexes and test strings); the durable fix was writing scripts with the Write tool. The merge into master was refused because another steward had files staged mid-commit, so the change went in as a commit by path of exactly its ten files, leaving that staged work untouched; the branch history is recorded in the commit message.

No restart is needed beyond the one already done. Open cockpit pages pick up the new button when they reload on the new build. The fresh-thread rollover for very long steward threads is deferred until a bounded digest can carry the open question, the interrupted-turn note and the files touched.
