# A failed landing rolled back the live tree (and the Star Chart vanished)

At 18:49 and 18:59 on 2026-10-04 two steward code jobs (the Cave's tile borders, Distribution's connection client) tried to land by running `git merge` in C:\dev\Anchor, the working tree the service, several stewards and Claude Code sessions all share. Both merges were refused because another steward had files staged mid-commit, and each failed merge left an "updating HEAD" entry in the reflog and rolled back the tree's uncommitted changes. Lost: settings.json (persona back to Jarvis from 15 September, so the Cave said "Chart Room" and the Star Chart seemed gone), rnd_registry.json (the Campaign, AI Club, Heterogeneity and Hierarchical Normalizing Flows projects dropped, so the Star Chart's own address opened a plain cockpit), and another session's unsaved work in anchor_gui.py, job_runner.py, anchor_healthcheck.py, static/project-window.js, two healthcheck tests, friction/index.json and general/RESTART.md. My own two failed merges at 17:35 (the responsiveness build) were of the same kind; that build was then committed by path, which is the safe pattern.

Recovered: the four projects from ids found in their cockpits' own records and the settings from this morning's values, then committed so a rollback cannot take them again (f255c1f); the two stuck jobs were landed once the index was clean (102 tests pass). Not recovered in source: the job-record summary speed-up and the healthcheck changes; session dev-5d saved the compiled job_runner, the disassembly of the lost functions and the surviving test file under Skill Foundry/planning/anchor-every-device-2026-10-03/rescue/, and the owning session was notified. Guards now: landing is frozen in code_jobs.land (work waits on its branch); the two Foundry jobs running under the old code had their branches renamed so their landing could not merge, were stopped by an idle-checked restart with their partial work committed to held branches, and were relaunched to continue from those branches.

The lesson is structural, not a slip: automation must never run merge, checkout, reset or stash in a shared working tree; landing must write only its own files and commit by path; runtime state should not be tracked by git; and cockpit folders should not live inside the product repository they change.
