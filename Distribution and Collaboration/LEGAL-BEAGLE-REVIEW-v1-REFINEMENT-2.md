## Operative wording checked

Section 1 expressly excludes permitted custom services from Sale: “Payment for the custom programming services permitted by section 4 is also not a Sale, including delivery of the resulting changes or modified copy solely for the customer's own use as part of those services.”

Section 3 retains the software-fee restriction: “The restriction includes a one-time price for a copy and recurring license, royalty, subscription or other fees for receiving or continuing to use a modified version.”

Section 4 identifies what payment buys: “Payment must buy programming services actually performed for that customer; it must not buy a software product, software-use rights or Hosted Access.” It also protects continued use: “The customer's exercise or retention of these permissions must not depend on continuing payments to the programmer.”

The strongest contrary reading is that the broad Sale definition or redistribution conditions could still treat required payment before delivery as a software charge. The reviewer found that the express delivery exception inside the Sale definition, section 3's recognition of permitted section 4 payments, and delivery under this license answer that reading. A fixed-price custom project still needs to be payment for actual programming services; calling a software price programming time does not exempt it. The customer's ordinary free permissions remain intact after delivery.

Sections 2, 5 and 9 were checked for consistency with this change. Free internal use, private changes, voluntary sharing and free forks remain. The review did not reopen Refinement 1's other corrections or individually accept the proposed supporting terms.
