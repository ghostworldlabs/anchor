# Ghost World Lab License v1, Refinement 2 — payment check

As of 4 October 2026. Textual and policy check only. Governing law is not selected. No enforceability opinion is offered. This check does not approve the final text, apply the license, or close the slice.

**Accepted policy.** Free personal and internal use, private modification, voluntary sharing, and free forks remain. Selling a covered-code product, or charging for hosted access, still needs separate written permission. The 4 October answer adds one permission: pay for customer-specific programming, including delivery of those changes for that customer’s own use. A one-time software-copy sale, and any recurring fee to keep using the modified version, stay outside the free license.

**Finding.** NO NEW MATERIAL PAYMENT-WORDING FINDINGS.

Sections 1, 3, 4, 5, and 9 fit that split. Compliant delivery sits outside “Sale.” Product resales, one-time copy charges, recurring version-use fees, and paid hosting stay unauthorized, including when renamed programming, maintenance, or support. Later labor may be billed when requested, and continued use cannot depend on paying. Companion row 4 and the contractor, later-work, one-time price, monthly-fee, bundle, and hosted-access examples say the same thing. Those tables are commentary, not operative terms.

**Operative fit.** Section 1 states: “Payment for the custom programming services permitted by section 4 is also not a Sale, including delivery of the resulting changes or modified copy solely for the customer's own use as part of those services. Neither exception authorizes a software-product sale, a fee for software-use rights or Paid Hosted Access.”

Section 3 still withholds a Sale, including “a one-time price for a copy and recurring license, royalty, subscription or other fees for receiving or continuing to use a modified version,” and withholds Paid Hosted Access. Its next sentence holds the line: section 4 payment “does not give the programmer permission to sell that version to other customers or charge for its software-use rights.”

Section 4 allows the bill only when payment buys “programming services actually performed for that customer” and does not buy “a software product, software-use rights or Hosted Access.” The copy is delivered “under this license.” “The customer's exercise or retention of these permissions must not depend on continuing payments.” Later fees exist only “when those services are requested,” and the same paragraph “does not permit recurring fees merely for access to, possession of or use of the modified version.” A “ready-made or generally offered” product stays under section 3 “even if some customization is included.”

Section 5’s “do not impose a fee or additional license restriction on the permissions” covers redistribution outside Internal Users. That prices or cuts the grant itself. Labor sold with the grant left intact still passes. Section 2 allows distribution “without a Sale,” and section 1 takes this delivery out of “Sale.” Under section 9, a real Sale or Paid Hosted Access is unauthorized when it occurs; cure does not create temporary commercial permission.

**Strongest contrary reading.** “Sale” still opens as “supplying a copy … or rights to use any of them in exchange for required payment,” and “a separate price of zero does not exempt a paid bundle.” Section 3 can be parsed so that any fee “for receiving” a modified version is banned. Section 5 can be parsed so that any sum required before delivery is a fee on the permissions. “Solely for the customer's own use” can be parsed as a downstream limit section 5 forbids the programmer to impose, or as so narrow that full-license delivery falls back into Sale. A fixed fee for a bespoke system is also close to a one-time copy sale where the invoice does not show labor actually performed.

The new sentences reject that reading. The exception is inside the Sale definition and names delivery. Section 3 treats section 4 payment as real and blocks onward sale and use-right charges. “Under this license” leaves the customer the ordinary free permissions, including a later unpaid fork. The fixed-price edge is the accepted labor-versus-sale line.

**Still unresolved, and not a failure of this check.** Owner, covered files, contributor and dependency rights, section 10 law and forum, patents, trademarks, liability, donations, and cure wording remain open supporting or adoption items. Refinement 1’s wage, confidentiality, rental, contribution, and cure wording was not reopened.
