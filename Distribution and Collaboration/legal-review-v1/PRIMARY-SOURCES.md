# Ghost World Lab License v1 — supplied authority excerpts

Retrieved from primary sources on 4 October 2026. These are explanatory authorities, not a selected governing jurisdiction or an opinion on enforceability. No case law is supplied or cited.

## Open Source Initiative: Open Source Definition

Source: https://opensource.org/osd

Section 1 states that a qualifying license “shall not restrict any party from selling” the software in the described distribution setting. Sections 3 and 6 concern derived works and use in fields including business. The candidate's no-sales restriction is incompatible with calling it OSI open source. This is a license-category comparison, not a statutory authority.

## Open Source Initiative: MIT License

Source: https://opensource.org/license/mit

The grant expressly includes “sell copies of the Software”. MIT also requires its copyright and permission notice in copies or substantial portions; it does not add a requirement to publish modifications. MIT therefore does not supply John's no-sales boundary.

## U.S. Copyright Office: What is Copyright?

Source: https://www.copyright.gov/what-is-copyright/

The Office describes exclusive rights including “Prepare derivative works based upon the work.” It describes ownership passing through “contracts like assignments”. This is U.S. explanatory guidance, not a case, and is not applied as governing law to this unplaced license. It supports asking for an ownership/authorization inventory rather than inferring ownership from a project or brand name.
