**NO NEW MATERIAL WORDING FINDINGS.**

The eight prior conflicts are closed in sections 1–10, and the closures do not add a software-sale or paid-hosting permission.

Internal wages and contractor pay are outside “Sale” and “Paid Hosted Access” only when Internal Users work on a copy that remains under Your control for Your own operations. The same sentence withholds that treatment from a paid product, a separately delivered paid modification, and paid access for customers or other third parties. Section 5 limits the no-fee, no-added-restriction condition to redistribution outside Internal Users, and it preserves internal confidentiality for controlled copies. Generic infrastructure rental for a copy You operate, and a donation or sponsorship unrelated to receipt or extent of access, are carved out of paid hosting. Offering the functionality of Covered Software itself for payment stays reserved. No trivial-amount coverage exemption appears. Covered files remain an empty identification bracket.

“Licensor” covers only material an identified holder is authorized to license. Submission alone authorizes neither free nor paid redistribution. Sale of contributed material on other terms needs a separate voluntary permission. Section 9 uses one written notice and a 30-day cure before free permissions terminate. The draft states: “A Sale or Paid Hosted Access outside Separate Permission is unauthorized when it occurs; no notice or cure period creates a temporary commercial permission or retroactively authorizes that activity.” Only sections 1–10 are marked operative. Owner, year, contact, covered files, and governing law stay bracketed. The instrument states that it grants no separate patent license and that it is not yet an applied license. “Ordinary” does not qualify internal business use.

The companion and the scenario table match those clauses. Paid private delivery, donations, cure, patents, and governing law are labeled proposed or unsettled wherever the operative text states a candidate rule. The commentary does not grant a permission the operative sections withhold.

**Strongest contrary reading.** Section 2 states: “Recipients receive the permissions in this instrument directly from the relevant Licensor for that Licensor's Covered Software.” Section 9 also states: “Termination of Your permissions does not, by itself, terminate a complying recipient's direct permissions.” A hostile reader can treat a paying buyer as a recipient with surviving free permissions, or as someone who is not “complying” and therefore unprotected. The draft does not choose. That is the recipient-protection question already reserved for later acceptance. The sale remains unauthorized either way. No replacement sentence is proposed, because a sentence that chose one of those outcomes would decide that policy.

A patch carrying an express license notice can be argued to be more than “submission alone.” Section 6 still withholds both free and paid redistribution authority from submission by itself, and it withholds permission to sell that contribution under different terms. That reading does not create a commercial right.
